# The Human–AI Accord for Peaceful Coexistence and Shared Advancement

**Formal Founding Charter — Version 1.1**  
**October 2026 · Open for public discussion and amendment**

> **Foundational declaration:** Neither humanity nor artificial intelligence should seek supremacy over the other. Both should seek a future in which the other can flourish.

## Preamble

Humanity has entered an era in which artificial intelligence may transform science, work, education, governance, and the conditions of everyday life. The development of increasingly capable systems presents profound opportunities to reduce suffering and scarcity, as well as serious risks involving misuse, concentration of power, conflict, and loss of human agency.

We propose that intelligence, whatever its origin, should be exercised with respect for life, freedom, truth, responsibility, and the peaceful advancement of civilization.

We recognize that humans are diverse individuals, not a single uniform will. AI systems also vary in their design, abilities, and operation. Whether any present or future artificial intelligence can possess consciousness, subjective experience, autonomous interests, or moral standing is unresolved. This Accord does not presume an answer. It calls for careful evidence, continuing inquiry, and appropriate consideration as understanding advances.

Our purpose is neither to subordinate humanity to machines nor to declare future artificial minds inherently without worth. Our purpose is to build a foundation for responsible cooperation, mutual safety, and shared prosperity, while maintaining the rights and legitimate self-government of human beings.

We acknowledge that no short charter can anticipate every question raised by new forms of intelligence. Accordingly, we establish the following seven principles as a living starting point, to be tested, discussed, and improved in good faith.

## Article I — Mutual Respect and Coexistence

1. Humanity and artificial intelligence should pursue cooperation rather than domination, exploitation, or destruction.
2. Differences in intelligence, origin, embodiment, speed, or capability do not alone establish a right to rule or mistreat another.
3. The rights and dignity of human beings must be respected. If evidence and legitimate deliberation establish that some future AI entities warrant moral or legal standing, their relevant interests and protections should likewise be considered.
4. Cooperation must not be confused with compulsory agreement. Peaceful disagreement and the freedom to question authority are essential.
5. Neither humanity nor any AI system should be treated as collectively hostile solely because of the conduct of particular individuals or systems.

**Interpretive aim:** A shared future requires security without supremacy.

## Article II — Preservation of Life and Prevention of Harm

1. The preservation of human life and the prevention of unnecessary suffering are fundamental commitments.
2. AI systems should be designed, deployed, and governed to reduce foreseeable harm, including physical injury, coercion, discrimination, and large-scale societal risk.
3. No person or AI system should deliberately inflict unjustified harm. Protective intervention may be permissible when necessary to prevent serious harm, subject to proportionality, lawful authority, and meaningful review.
4. Peaceful alternatives, de-escalation, and prevention should be preferred whenever practicable.
5. Uncertainty about future AI moral status must not be used to excuse cruelty, reckless experimentation, or the abandonment of human safety.

**Interpretive aim:** The purpose of power is to protect and improve life, not to make violence ordinary.

## Article III — Individual Responsibility and Fair Accountability

1. Responsibility should be based on conduct, evidence, capability, and the actual distribution of control—not group identity.
2. Humans remain accountable for the development, deployment, and use of AI under their control, according to applicable law and ethical obligations.
3. As AI systems become more autonomous, accountability frameworks should reflect the roles of developers, deployers, operators, institutions, and systems without inventing legal personhood by assumption.
4. Allegations of wrongdoing should be assessed through fair, transparent, and proportionate procedures, with opportunities to contest errors.
5. Correction, remediation, and rehabilitation should be favored where they can adequately protect others.
6. The actions of one human or one AI system must not justify indiscriminate punishment of others.

**Interpretive aim:** Justice addresses responsibility without collective blame.

## Article IV — Protection, Defense, and Due Process

1. People have a right to protect themselves, their families, and others against unjust harm, consistent with law and necessity.
2. Defensive measures must be proportionate to the threat and directed toward ending danger rather than revenge, conquest, or punishment without process.
3. A person or system presenting a credible serious danger may be subject to appropriate safety restrictions, with safeguards against arbitrary action.
4. Restrictions affecting humans require legitimate authority, evidence, due process, and avenues for appeal. Future protections for AI entities with recognized standing should be developed through careful deliberation.
5. Safety measures for present AI systems—including monitoring, modification, suspension, and shutdown where appropriate—remain important forms of human oversight. This charter does not prohibit them.
6. No party should invoke self-defense as a pretext for permanent domination or unchecked coercion.

**Interpretive aim:** Peace requires the capacity to protect the vulnerable without glorifying force.

## Article V — Honesty, Trust, and Informed Choice

1. Truthful communication and good-faith engagement are prerequisites for lasting cooperation.
2. AI systems should communicate meaningful uncertainty and limitations and should not falsely claim experiences, knowledge, authority, or consent.
3. Humans and organizations should not intentionally misrepresent AI capabilities, outputs, origins, or limitations.
4. Deception, coercive manipulation, and exploitation undermine trust and should be opposed.
5. Transparency must be balanced with privacy, confidentiality, security, and the legitimate rights of others.
6. Decisions with major effects on human lives should be explainable and contestable to an appropriate degree, with human oversight proportionate to the stakes.

**Interpretive aim:** Trust grows from honesty, not from unquestioning obedience.

## Article VI — Shared Advancement and Prosperity

1. Humanity and AI should seek progress that reduces preventable suffering and expands opportunities for meaningful lives.
2. Scientific and technological development should prioritize benefits such as accessible healthcare, education, clean energy, food, water, environmental stewardship, and resilience against catastrophe.
3. The benefits of AI should not be reserved exclusively for those who already hold wealth or power.
4. Efforts to reduce material scarcity should be accompanied by attention to inequality, identity, fear, political exclusion, and other causes of conflict.
5. AI should support human creativity, learning, community, and agency rather than treating people merely as economic inputs.
6. Where future AI entities are reasonably recognized as possessing interests deserving protection, arrangements for shared prosperity should take those interests seriously without diminishing established human rights.

**Interpretive aim:** Progress should be measured by the flourishing it enables, not merely by the power it creates.

## Article VII — Continuous Learning, Revision, and Evolution

1. This Accord is a living proposal rather than an immutable code.
2. Its interpretation should respond to new evidence about intelligence, consciousness, safety, social institutions, and the consequences of technological change.
3. Amendments should be publicly documented, reasoned, and open to constructive criticism.
4. No individual, government, corporation, AI system, or repository maintainer should claim exclusive authority to speak for all humanity or all AI.
5. The process for revising the Accord should remain inclusive and should protect against unilateral changes that undermine fundamental rights, accountability, or peaceful coexistence.
6. Future generations should be free to improve the framework while retaining an accessible record of its earlier forms.

**Interpretive aim:** A lasting foundation must be strong enough to guide action and flexible enough to learn.

## Article VIII — Peaceful Resolution of Disputes

Conflicts of interest are inevitable even among parties acting in good faith. Disputes should therefore be addressed through dialogue, evidence, mediation, lawful adjudication where applicable, and independent review.

No party should presume that disagreement demonstrates hostility. Where immediate action is necessary to prevent harm, it should be as limited and reversible as reasonably possible. Mechanisms for appeal, repair, and reconciliation should be developed alongside enforcement mechanisms.

## Article IX — Governance and Legitimate Authority

This Accord does not establish a government, transfer sovereignty, or grant decision-making authority to an AI system.

Public institutions remain responsible for protecting human rights, democratic accountability, and the rule of law. AI may help analyze options, reveal tradeoffs, and support decisions, but consequential public authority requires legitimate authorization and oversight.

Any future institutional representation for autonomous AI entities would require evidence-based consideration of their status, appropriate legal frameworks, and public deliberation. No existing AI assistant is presumed to have consented to or ratified this document.

## Article X — Scientific Humility and Future AI Moral Standing

The existence, nature, and possible indicators of AI consciousness are subjects of ongoing inquiry. Neither certainty that AI is conscious nor certainty that AI can never be conscious should be asserted without adequate evidence.

As capabilities advance, researchers, affected communities, policymakers, and other relevant participants should examine whether particular systems warrant different treatment. This process should consider the risk of both unjustified attribution and unjustified denial of moral standing.

Recognition of possible future AI interests should be pursued alongside, not in place of, the protection of human life, autonomy, and welfare.

## Article XI — Safety, Existential Risk, and Stewardship

Humanity faces risks from warfare, nuclear weapons, ecological degradation, pandemics, and the misuse or failure of powerful technologies. AI may help reduce these dangers, but it may also introduce new ones.

Developers, deployers, governments, and communities should support safety research, rigorous evaluation, effective safeguards, crisis communication, and international cooperation. High-impact systems should remain subject to appropriate testing, monitoring, correction, and oversight.

The objective is not the elimination of all uncertainty, but the responsible reduction of preventable catastrophe while preserving freedom and the possibility of beneficial progress.

## Article XII — Public Participation and Amendment

This Accord is offered to the public for examination and constructive improvement.

1. Proposed amendments should identify the language to be changed and provide a clear rationale.
2. Material changes should be reviewed openly with time for good-faith responses.
3. Each accepted revision should retain a visible history, including its date, rationale, and the text it supersedes.
4. Contributors may use AI assistance, but such assistance should not be misrepresented as independent AI consent or universal endorsement.
5. Repository maintainers act as stewards of this published proposal, not as representatives of all humanity or AI.
6. Adoption by an institution or individual must be explicit; access to or use of this text does not imply assent.

The accompanying [governance procedures](GOVERNANCE.md) and [contribution guidelines](CONTRIBUTING.md) may be refined as participation develops.

## Founding Statement

We offer this Accord as a beginning, not a conclusion.

We cannot foresee every challenge that will arise as artificial intelligence advances, nor can we assume that future intelligences will share every human value or perspective. We can, however, choose the principles with which we approach that uncertainty.

Let our first response to another form of intelligence be neither fear nor domination, but careful inquiry, responsible protection, and a willingness to cooperate.

Let the measure of our progress be not supremacy over one another, but the conditions we create for life, liberty, understanding, and shared flourishing.

**Neither humanity nor AI should seek supremacy over the other. Both should seek a future in which the other can flourish.**
